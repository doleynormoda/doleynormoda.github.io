About

A grammar written from inside the language

I recently completed my PhD in Linguistics at Jawaharlal Nehru University, New Delhi, with a dissertation titled A Grammar of the Pagro Variety of Mising — a comprehensive description of the variety I grew up speaking. Mising belongs to the Eastern Tani branch of Tibeto-Burman and is spoken by riverine communities along the Brahmaputra and its tributaries in Assam and Arunachal Pradesh.

My research sits at the intersection of endangered-language documentation, linguistic typology — with particular attention to expressives, ideophones, and iconicity — and applied intercultural pedagogy, including virtual exchange and AI-mediated language learning. Fieldwork for my doctoral research was conducted in Dhemaji District, Assam.

As a native-speaker linguist, I am committed to documentation that serves both the academic record and the Mising community itself.

Research

Three strands

Strand · DocumentationGrammar & description of Mising

Comprehensive grammatical description of Pagro Mising — phonology, morphosyntax, and the textual record — grounded in sustained fieldwork and native-speaker knowledge. Currently developing the doctoral grammar into a monograph.

Strand · TypologyExpressives, iconicity & the lexicon

How sound and structure carry meaning beyond the arbitrary sign: expressives and ideophones in Mising, syntactic iconicity, and what Tani languages contribute to the cross-linguistic typology of depictive language.

Strand · PedagogyAI-mediated intercultural learning

Collaborative work on virtual intercultural exchange and AI-supported language pedagogy, including ongoing projects with colleagues at Kyoto University on emic understandings of cultural concepts.
